# Security Properties Model

**Status:** Normative

This document defines the security-property vocabulary and claim model used by E2EESA.

A security property is not a bare feature flag. Every claim is meaningful only within an explicit scope, against an explicit threat model, for identified assets, and subject to stated assumptions and limitations.

## 1. Claim model

A production security-property claim MUST identify:

- the security-property ID;
- the scope in which the property is claimed;
- the protected asset or behavior to which the property applies;
- the applicable threat IDs;
- applicable composite-scenario IDs when relevant;
- the temporal phase or phases in which the claim is intended to hold;
- prerequisites and trust assumptions;
- limitations and explicitly excluded cases; and
- the evidence class required by the applicable assurance profile once assurance profiles are defined.

A claim MUST NOT be interpreted more broadly than these fields establish.

## 2. Claim states

E2EESA recognizes the following claim states:

- **provided** — the implementation asserts that the property holds within the stated scope and threat model;
- **conditional** — the property is asserted only when explicitly stated conditions are satisfied;
- **not-claimed** — the implementation makes no claim that the property holds;
- **not-applicable** — the property does not apply to the declared product capability or scope.

A property marked `not-claimed` MUST NOT be inferred from another claimed property.

A property marked `not-applicable` MUST include enough scope information to establish why it does not apply.

## 3. Temporal phases

Where compromise can change a property's status, claims MUST use one or more of these phases:

- **pre-compromise**;
- **during-compromise**;
- **post-compromise-pre-healing**;
- **post-healing**; and
- **steady-state** for properties not meaningfully expressed around a compromise event.

Claims of forward secrecy or post-compromise security MUST use compromise-aware phases rather than only `steady-state`.

## 4. Baseline property classes

### SP-CONFIDENTIALITY: Protected-content confidentiality

Unauthorized parties cannot obtain protected-content plaintext within the stated threat model.

A confidentiality claim MUST identify the protected assets and intended recipients.

Content confidentiality MUST NOT be interpreted as metadata confidentiality.

A service-provider confidentiality claim MUST state whether `TM-SERVICE-READ` and `TM-SERVICE-ACTIVE` are covered.

### SP-INTEGRITY: Protected-content integrity

Unauthorized modification of protected content is detected or rejected within the stated threat model.

The claim MUST identify whether detection occurs before application use, before user presentation, or only retrospectively.

### SP-MESSAGE-AUTHENTICITY: Message authenticity

A recipient obtains assurance that a protected message was generated by an authorized sender or sender endpoint according to the selected protocol.

This property MUST NOT be interpreted as transferable third-party proof unless non-repudiation is separately claimed.

### SP-PEER-AUTHENTICATION: Peer or endpoint authentication

A participant obtains assurance about the identity or authorization of a communicating peer, device, or endpoint.

The claim MUST state the identity granularity being authenticated: user, account, device, endpoint, or another explicitly defined principal.

### SP-AUTHORIZATION-INTEGRITY: Authorization integrity

Security-relevant authorization decisions cannot be changed or exercised by an unauthorized principal without detection or rejection within the stated scope.

Examples include device enrollment, group membership, recovery authorization, and privileged administrative actions.

### SP-FORWARD-SECRECY: Forward secrecy

Compromise of specified current or long-term secret material does not expose designated past protected content beyond the stated exposure window.

A forward-secrecy claim MUST identify:

- the compromise type;
- the secret material assumed exposed;
- the protected historical interval;
- any retained ciphertext or state needed by the adversary; and
- exclusions such as plaintext retained by endpoints or backups.

Forward secrecy MUST NOT be claimed against live endpoint compromise merely because ratcheting is present.

### SP-POST-COMPROMISE-SECURITY: Post-compromise security

After a specified compromise ends, protocol evolution can restore one or more stated security properties for later communications.

A PCS claim MUST identify:

- the compromise being recovered from;
- the property or properties restored;
- the healing event;
- the maximum or expected exposure window if one can be defined;
- required honest-party activity; and
- conditions that prevent healing.

PCS MUST NOT be represented as protection while an adversary retains unrestricted live control of the endpoint state required for healing.

### SP-KEY-CONSISTENCY: Key or state consistency

Honest participants can prevent or detect materially inconsistent views of identity keys, device state, membership state, or other cryptographic directory state within the stated model.

A claim MUST identify the consistency object and whether the mechanism provides prevention, detection, or both.

### SP-DOWNGRADE-RESISTANCE: Downgrade resistance

An adversary cannot cause participants to use a weaker protocol, algorithm, profile, version, or capability than their applicable minimum security policy permits without detection or failure.

The claim MUST identify the negotiated or selected object and the minimum-policy source.

### SP-REPLAY-RESISTANCE: Replay resistance

Unauthorized replay of previously valid protocol data is rejected or rendered harmless within the stated scope.

The claim MUST identify the replayed object and expected handling of duplicates, reordered traffic, and state rollback where relevant.

### SP-ROLLBACK-RESISTANCE: State rollback resistance

An adversary cannot cause an implementation to accept security-relevant stale state in a manner that violates a claimed property, or such rollback is detected according to the stated model.

The claim MUST identify the protected state and continuity mechanism.

### SP-DENIABILITY: Deniability

Protocol evidence is intentionally insufficient, within the defined model, to provide transferable cryptographic proof to an uninvolved third party that a particular participant authored a particular communication.

A deniability claim MUST identify the evidence available to the third party and the parties assumed capable of simulation or forgery.

Deniability MUST NOT be inferred merely from the absence of digital signatures.

### SP-NON-REPUDIATION: Non-repudiation

The system intentionally provides durable evidence attributable to a principal such that authorship, approval, or another defined act can be demonstrated to a third party under the stated model.

A non-repudiation claim MUST identify the evidence, verification method, identity binding, and trust assumptions.

Deniability and non-repudiation MAY both exist in one product only when their scopes are clearly separated. They MUST NOT be claimed simultaneously for the same evidence and act under the same scope.

### SP-METADATA-MINIMIZATION: Metadata minimization

The system reduces collection, retention, disclosure, or linkability of explicitly identified metadata relative to a stated baseline.

A metadata-minimization claim MUST identify the metadata classes addressed and the observer against which minimization is evaluated.

The phrase "metadata protected" MUST NOT be used without identifying which metadata fields and what protection is provided.

### SP-METADATA-CONFIDENTIALITY: Metadata confidentiality

Specified metadata is concealed from specified adversaries within the stated threat model.

This property is distinct from minimizing metadata creation or retention.

### SP-UNLINKABILITY: Unlinkability

Specified events, identifiers, messages, or actions cannot be reliably linked to the same principal or relationship by a stated adversary beyond a defined advantage or information baseline.

An unlinkability claim MUST define the items whose linkage is protected and the observer's available auxiliary information.

### SP-AVAILABILITY: Availability

A defined service or operation remains usable under explicitly bounded failure or attack conditions.

Availability MUST be scoped separately from confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity.

An implementation MUST NOT claim general availability against an adversary assumed able to suppress all communication indefinitely.

### SP-RECOVERY-CONFIDENTIALITY: Recovery confidentiality

Use or compromise of the ordinary recovery mechanism does not disclose protected content beyond the limits explicitly stated by the recovery profile.

The claim MUST identify recovery authorities, secrets, services, hardware, and historical content affected.

### SP-BACKUP-CONFIDENTIALITY: Backup confidentiality

Stored backup material does not reveal protected content to the specified backup adversary without the required recovery/decryption authority.

Live-channel E2EE MUST NOT be used as evidence for this property without separate backup analysis.

### SP-SOFTWARE-INTEGRITY: Software and update integrity

Unauthorized or unapproved modification or substitution of security-relevant software, configuration, or artifacts is prevented or detected within the stated supply-chain model.

The claim MUST state whether malicious-but-authorized signed updates are in or out of scope.

### SP-BUILD-PROVENANCE: Build provenance verifiability

A relying party can verify defined provenance information about the production of an artifact, subject to the trust assumptions of the provenance system.

This property MUST NOT be interpreted as proof that the source code itself is secure.

### SP-PQ-CONFIDENTIALITY: Post-quantum confidentiality

Specified protected content remains confidential against the declared post-quantum adversary model.

The claim MUST identify whether it covers `TM-QUANTUM-HARVEST`, `TM-QUANTUM-ACTIVE`, or both, and MUST identify any classical components whose failure would still defeat confidentiality.

### SP-PQ-AUTHENTICATION: Post-quantum authentication

Specified authentication properties remain valid against the declared active quantum adversary model.

Post-quantum confidentiality MUST NOT be interpreted as post-quantum authentication.

## 5. Relationships between properties

Security properties are independent unless the standard explicitly defines a dependency.

In particular:

- confidentiality does not imply integrity;
- integrity does not imply sender authenticity;
- message authenticity does not imply non-repudiation;
- E2EE content confidentiality does not imply metadata confidentiality;
- forward secrecy does not imply post-compromise security;
- post-compromise security does not imply protection during live endpoint compromise;
- post-quantum confidentiality does not imply post-quantum authentication;
- transport security does not imply E2EE;
- backup confidentiality does not follow automatically from live-channel confidentiality;
- build provenance does not prove source-code correctness.

Profiles MUST NOT derive an unclaimed property solely from another property unless a later normative E2EESA rule expressly establishes that implication.

## 6. Threat coverage

Property definitions identify the meaning of a property, not universal threat coverage.

A profile MUST declare the threat IDs under which each `provided` or `conditional` property is claimed.

The same property MAY be provided against one threat and not claimed against another.

## 7. Composite threats

Where a composite scenario from the unified threat model materially changes a property, the profile MUST declare the property's status for that composite scenario.

A property that survives each constituent threat independently MUST NOT be assumed to survive their composition.

## 8. Assumptions and prerequisites

A security-property claim MUST enumerate assumptions on which it depends when those assumptions are not E2EESA invariants.

Examples include:

- uncompromised endpoint execution;
- trustworthy secure hardware;
- availability of an external transparency auditor;
- honest threshold participants;
- uncompromised software-update roots;
- fresh entropy;
- trusted time within a stated tolerance; or
- successful user verification.

Hidden assumptions invalidate the completeness of the claim.

## 9. Limitations

A property claim MUST record material limitations.

Limitations include exposure windows, excluded data classes, unsupported clients, legacy modes, recovery exceptions, metadata still visible, and threats explicitly out of scope.

Marketing language MUST NOT broaden a claim beyond these limitations.

## 10. Evidence

This PR defines the claim vocabulary, not the assurance level required to substantiate each claim.

Later assurance profiles will define acceptable evidence such as test results, source review, interoperability evidence, formal analysis, reproducible builds, independent audits, or operational monitoring.

Until then, the existence of a property definition in E2EESA MUST NOT be treated as evidence that an implementation provides that property.

## 11. Machine-readable claims

Machine-readable property claims MUST conform to the security-property claim schema.

Unknown property IDs MUST fail validation once the property registry is in scope.

Unknown threat or composite-scenario IDs MUST fail validation.

A claim with state `provided` or `conditional` MUST identify at least one threat ID and at least one protected asset or behavior.

A `conditional` claim MUST state at least one condition.

## 12. Fail-closed interpretation

If a claim omits a dimension required by the property's definition, the omitted dimension MUST NOT be silently assumed.

If a claim is ambiguous about threat coverage, temporal coverage, protected assets, or scope, the narrower interpretation controls until the ambiguity is resolved.
